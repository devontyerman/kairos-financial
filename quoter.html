What is whole life insurance?

Permanent protection that never expires.

Unlike term insurance — which only covers you for a set number of years and then ends — whole life insurance lasts your entire life, as long as your premiums are paid. It's the kind of coverage families turn to so a funeral, final medical bills, or leftover debts never become a burden for the people they leave behind.

A portion of every premium also builds cash value that grows over time and belongs to you — money you can borrow against if life ever calls for it.

Good to know

Common questions

How much coverage do I actually need? +
Most people choose enough to cover their final expenses — a funeral and burial average $8,000–$12,000 in 2026 — plus any leftover medical bills or small debts. Coverage typically ranges from $5,000 up to $50,000. Your Kairos agent can help you land on the right amount for your situation.
Do I have to take a medical exam? +
In most cases, no. Whole life final-expense plans are typically "simplified issue," meaning you answer a few health questions instead of taking a medical exam. Many applicants are approved within minutes.
Will my premium ever go up? +
No. With whole life, the premium you're quoted is locked in for life. It won't increase as you age or if your health changes — and your coverage won't expire as long as premiums are paid.
What's the difference between this and term insurance? +
Term insurance covers you for a fixed number of years and then ends, with no cash value. Whole life lasts your entire life, keeps the same premium, and builds cash value you can borrow against. Most people outlive their term policies — whole life is designed to always be there.
How fast does my family get the money? +
Once a claim is filed with a death certificate, benefits are usually paid to your beneficiaries quickly — often within days. The payout is generally income-tax-free and can be used for anything they need.
